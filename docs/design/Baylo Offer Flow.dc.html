Turn 7 · Offer flow · Direction B
Direction B — the two columns
Where A reads the gap as one horizontal track, B stands the two sides up next to each other: your side left, theirs right, a hairline down the middle, both bottom-aligned so the difference is the empty space above your column. Settling isn't a radio choice, it's stacking — Leaves and promises drop in as extra blocks until your column reaches theirs, and the button stays inert until it does or you deliberately send short. Same palette and copy voice as A; different claim about what the gap is.

Turn 6 · Offer flow · Direction A
Direction A — the bar
One scrolling screen, not a wizard — offering, gap, settlement, message and hub are sections split by hairlines, so the gap is visible in the same glance as the item that caused it. The gap itself is a single 358px track: their value is the whole track, your offer fills it from the left in green, and what's left is terracotta. The number above it is 44px mono. Nothing about the treatment changes between a 40-Leaf gap and a 1,900-Leaf one except how much terracotta you see, which is the honest answer.

Row 1: 6a pick your item, 6b roughly even, 6c you're offering more, 6d small gap, 6e large gap, 6f very large gap, 6g DPA proposal, 6h DPA keyboard-up, 6i message and hub, 6j owner accepting a DPA. Row 2 states: 6k no items, 6l not verified, 6m tier too low, 6n offer already pending, 6o sending, 6p send failed.
